How the records are harmonized
  1. Clean. Vegetation fires only (FIRMS type 0); MODIS confidence ≥ 30, VIIRS nominal/high.
  2. Common grid. Detections are binned into 0.1° cells per day. A cell with ≥ 1 detection is a fire cell-day, which removes most of the 1 km vs 375 m pixel-size effect.
  3. Calibrate locally. Over 2012+ both sensors saw the same fires. For your area we fit VIIRS ÷ MODIS per calendar month, shrunk toward the area ratio and then toward a worldwide ratio, so small or quiet areas stay stable.
  4. Three eras. Terra-only (2000–Jul 2002) and Terra+Aqua (to Jan 2012) are scaled to VIIRS-equivalent; VIIRS is used as-is after that.
  5. Validate. Leave-one-year-out cross-validation predicts each VIIRS year from MODIS alone.
  6. Live. The last week of VIIRS S-NPP near-real-time detections is compared with the same dates in every past year (static industrial sources masked).

Data: NASA FIRMS — MODIS C6.1, VIIRS S-NPP 375 m (archive + NRT). Boundaries: Natural Earth.

    Welcome to FireCal

    Satellites have watched fires since 2000, but the older MODIS sensors and the newer, sharper VIIRS sensor count them differently. FireCal harmonizes both into one consistent 24-year burning calendar for any place on Earth.

    1. Pick an area. Search a country, click it on the map, tap ◎ Me, or Draw area for a custom box. A country opened for the first time takes a minute or two to load; after that it's instant.
    2. Check this week. The Early warning card compares the last week of live NASA detections with the same dates in every past year.
    3. Read the calendar. Every month since 2000 appears as one square: darker means more burning. Switch to Anomaly to spot unusual months, and tap a row to open that year day by day.
    4. Plan. Critical periods show when the season opens, peaks and closes. What this means summarizes it for responders, land managers or scientists.
    5. Share. Every view has its own link. Print a report or download the data as CSV.

    A fire cell-day is one 0.1° grid cell (≈ 11 km) with at least one satellite fire detection that day. Press / to search.
